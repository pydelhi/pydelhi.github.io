PyDelhi has a new look!

{% photo "blog/new-pydelhi-website-cover.png", "The PyDelhi logo above three pastel cards and the green snake", "(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" %}

If you're reading this, you've already found your way to the new website.

## Why the redesign?

I'd been hearing the same feedback for a while: the old website looked outdated and didn't really tell you much about PyDelhi. If you were new to the community and wanted to find the next meetup, learn how to give a talk, or figure out who to reach out to, you'd have to dig around a bit. The logo wasn't helping much either. It felt like it belonged to another decade.

And honestly, none of that was surprising. The old site had never really been designed with much intention. It did the job, but it was definitely due for a refresh. Since I was the only designer on the team, I took on the redesign, and I was pretty happy to get the chance to give PyDelhi a more considered visual identity.

## The logo

I started with the logo, since it would set the direction for everything else. It's hand-drawn, and the idea behind it is pretty simple: bring Python and Delhi together in one mark.

The blue part is a snake, inspired by Python's familiar logo. The golden part underneath takes inspiration from Delhi's Mughal architecture, particularly the grand gates and their arched windows. Python on top, Delhi underneath. That's pretty much the whole idea.

## The colours

The blue and yellow are inspired by Python's own logo, but I tweaked both a little. The blue became a deeper navy, and the yellow a warmer gold. They just worked better that way.

Navy is our primary colour for text and headings. Gold is used for fills, highlights and underlines, but never for text on a light background. As nice as it looks, it's just not readable enough on its own.

Then there are the pastels: soft blue, yellow and green. Partly because I like pastels, and partly because they work really well with the navy and gold. They also turned out to be useful for more than just decoration.

On the Volunteer page, each colour represents a different kind of contribution: blue for things you do before a meetup, yellow for things you do on the day, and green for things you can help with anytime. The About page uses different tints to distinguish between event types, too. Each pastel has a darker shade of the same colour for its text, so everything stays readable without losing the softer look.

## The snake

You might have noticed a little green snake popping up across the website. That's our Python mascot, and it likes to wander around.

It appears at the top of some pages, loops around titles, and slithers along the bottom of the footer. It's not there to explain anything or serve a particular purpose. It's just a little bit of personality for the site. Every community deserves a mascot that can hang around without having to justify its existence.

## What's new on the site?

A lot of the redesign was about making the website more useful, especially if you're new to PyDelhi and want to get involved.

- **A more useful homepage.** The next meetup is front and centre, with events pulled directly from Commudle. That means we don't have to manually update the website every time we announce a new event. There's also a graph with a square for every month we've held a meetup since 2011, which is a nice way to see just how long this community has been around.
- **Dedicated pages for getting involved.** About, Community, Speak, Volunteer, Sponsor and the Code of Conduct each have their own page, with a clear focus. About walks you through PyDelhi's growth as a timeline. Speak breaks down the four stages a talk goes through. Volunteer introduces the core team and the different volunteer teams. Sponsor explains the ways you can support us and showcases the logos of those who've already helped out.
- **A proper blog.** All the recaps from the old website have been moved over, so none of that history is lost. And this post is the newest addition.

## Thanks, mostly to opinions

I designed and built the website, but other volunteers helped along the way by sharing feedback, weighing in on ideas and helping settle all the little decisions that inevitably come up during a redesign. That input made the final result a lot better.

And, of course, a redesign is never really finished. If something looks off, a link doesn't work, or something reads awkwardly, let us know. Those things are easy enough to fix.
